# Riaya-Net: our own model

A small neural network (17 inputs → 24 → 12 → 1, ReLU) that we built and trained ourselves.

1. **Pre-trained** on our simulated cohort: 20,000 men generated by `synth.simulate()`. Every effect in the simulation (smoking, BMI, sleep, stress, sitting, heat, steroids, varicocele and more) comes from a cited study in our evidence graph.
2. **Fine-tuned** on real data: UCI Fertility (Gil et al. 2012; 100 men with a WHO semen analysis, 12 abnormal). Fine-tuning continues training at a lower learning rate, with 400 simulated rows replayed each epoch so the network keeps what it learned. Inputs the real data doesn't record are filled with the cohort average.
3. **Runs on the phone**: the weights are 9 KB of JSON (`web/models/riayanet.json`), and `web/js/riayanet.js` runs the same network in the browser. A test checks that the browser and the server give the same answer.

Code: `backend/app/riayanet.py` (`python -m app.riayanet` from `backend/` re-runs the evaluation and retrains). Tests: `backend/tests/test_riayanet.py`.

## How well it works (honest)

Repeated stratified 5-fold cross-validation on the real data (10 repeats, 50 folds). AUC is the mean, with the 2.5th–97.5th percentile across folds.

| Model | AUC | Interval | Brier |
|---|---|---|---|
| Logistic regression, real data only (our existing model) | 0.645 | 0.28–0.97 | 0.222 |
| Same network, trained from scratch on real data | 0.679 | 0.40–0.94 | 0.167 |
| Riaya-Net, pre-trained only (no real data) | 0.568 | 0.27–0.83 | 0.171 |
| **Riaya-Net, pre-trained + fine-tuned** | **0.592** | 0.30–0.84 | 0.203 |

On the simulated hold-out, AUC was 0.647 before fine-tuning and 0.633 after, so fine-tuning didn't make it forget the evidence.

Fine-tuning settings we tried (real-data AUC): 40 epochs, learning rate 0.0008, replay 400 → 0.592 (kept); 0.002/100 → 0.599; 80 epochs 0.002/50 → 0.574; 60 epochs 0.003/25 → 0.588; 30 epochs 0.001/no replay → 0.608. We kept the planned setting rather than the best-scoring one, because choosing by these scores would overfit 12 positive cases.

**What this means.** With 100 real men and 12 abnormal results, no model here is clearly better than another; the intervals overlap almost completely. Pre-training on the simulated cohort doesn't help on this dataset. One likely reason is that the real data records different things (season, fever, childhood illness, surgery) from the factors our evidence graph is strongest on (BMI, sleep, stress, heat, steroids), which UCI doesn't record.

**Why it's still in the product.** It is the pipeline we will use when real Abu Dhabi pilot data arrives (with WHO semen analysis and the full questionnaire): pre-train on the evidence, fine-tune on local men, check in cross-validation, and run on the phone. Until then it is shown to clinicians only, as a second opinion with each factor's contribution, never to the man and never as a basis for triage. Triage stays rule-based and guideline-cited.
